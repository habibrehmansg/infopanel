# USB panels

The engine drives small USB screens (panels) itself: each shows one profile, live, as the preview does. BeadaPanel is the first family; others follow.

## What is driven

- **BeadaPanel**, every model InfoPanel 1.4 knows (21; their ids, `2` to `Z`, are in `InfoPanel.Core/Panels/BeadaPanel/BeadaPanelModel.cs`), over USB through libusb-1.0, as InfoPanel 1.4 speaks to them byte for byte: the info query, the start sequence, frames in RGB565, brightness, and the screen turned off when a panel stops. Panels known to decode JPEG get JPEG frames instead (below), about twice the frame rate on a 1920 × 480 panel.
- **libusb-1.0** is looked for, by full path: beside the engine, then in the folder `INFOPANEL_LIBUSB_DIR` names (a full path), then on macOS in `/opt/homebrew/lib` and `/usr/local/lib`, and on Linux the system's `libusb-1.0.so.0`. Without it panels are unsupported, with one line in the log, and nothing else changes. The Mac app and the Windows installer carry it beside the engine ([packaging.md](packaging.md)); an engine built from source on macOS finds it after `brew install libusb`.
- **Another program holding a panel** keeps it: the engine never resets a device or takes it from a driver, sets its configuration only when it has none, and a panel it can't claim is tried again every second with one line in the log.
- **`--no-panels`** turns all of it off: no USB device is listed, opened or written.

A family is a driver behind one small interface, in the `InfoPanel.Panels.Sdk` assembly (.NET 10): `IPanelDriver` names the USB vendor and product ids it drives, tells its own panels by a probe and opens a session; `IPanelSession` writes a frame and answers the time the device took for it (the pace learns from that time), sets the brightness, and closes with the screen turned off within the stop's budget. The engine lists the devices and offers each only to the drivers whose ids match it, in order: the first whose probe answers "mine" keeps it until it is unplugged, one that couldn't ask (busy, a probe that throws, or one still waiting on the device at its 5 s deadline) is offered again a second later, and a device no driver names is never opened. A probe is cut short when the engine stops or the computer sleeps, and a session that fails to close still lets the panel go. The engine still renders, turns, encodes and paces every frame, keeps `devices.json` and answers the API. BeadaPanel's driver is built into the engine (`BeadaPanelDriver`, its protocol unchanged); every other family comes from a panel package ([plugins](plugins.md#panel-packages)), and the log names the driver that takes a device (`driver beadapanel, built in`, `driver turing, the panel package InfoPanel.Panels.Turing`).

A package's drivers run in a panel host, a process of its own (the engine executable with `--panel-host`, one a package, run as the user over a current-user pipe), started when a device its families name is plugged in and stopped after the last one leaves. To the engine a hosted driver is one more `IPanelSession`, a proxy (`InfoPanel.Core/Panels/Hosted/`), so one runner and one pacer serve both kinds. No frame crosses the pipe: each session has a file of two frame slots in the run folder, private to the user, which the engine writes and the host maps read-only; a `write` message hands a slot to the host until its answer, the engine filling the other meanwhile, and with both still held a frame is refused as a timeout. A write the host hasn't answered within 5 s is a timeout too. Every call goes through the host's connection as it is then: a host that ends unasked is started again after 2 s, at most three times in a minute, and its panels' sessions start again on the new one (the reason `driver` meanwhile); after the third the package fails and its panels are `failed` · `driver`. A package turned off leaves its panels `off` · `driver`, and a saved panel whose family no driver declares is `failed` · `driver`. On quit the panels close within the 3 s bound, then each host is asked to end in what is left of it, and killed past it. A spare engine can list virtual devices in place of the computer's (the `Engine:VirtualPanels` setting, a count) for the virtual panel package, a test fixture that is never shipped (`InfoPanel.Panels.Virtual`, family `virtual`, device `1209:0001`).

## How it runs

- Every second the engine lists the USB devices. A BeadaPanel it hasn't seen is identified by its info query and matched to the panels it saved, by serial, else (a panel without one) by its USB port. Panels that report the same serial are told apart by the port each was last seen at; one that still can't be told apart (at a port none of them had) is listed as a new panel, with a line in the log. A new one is saved **off, without a profile**: the log says `BeadaPanel {model} found, serial …, at {port}: saved, off`.
- A saved panel runs while it is plugged in, turned on, and its profile exists and can be drawn. It shows the profile at its own size: turned by its rotation, scaled to fit and centred, the rest black. Frames come at the engine's frame rate (Settings › Engine), slowed to what the panel takes, and the panel's own thread keeps to that pace at every write, whatever the frame rate. A panel keeps frames of its own before it shows them, and a write returns once the panel has taken the frame, not when it is shown; once that store is full, each write waits for the panel to show a frame, and every change is shown that whole store late (seconds on a BeadaPanel 8). The engine can't see how full the store is until it is full, so it stays under what the panel is known to show and only the writes lower it: a T113 panel (the BeadaPanel 8 and its kin) is sent at most 15 frames a second as JPEG and 12 raw, under the 17–20 and 16 it was measured to show; any panel is sent at most 85 % of what its thread can write (a panel that needs 48 ms a frame is sent at most 17 a second), so a stream whose writes are long by nature, as raw frames are, stays under what the panel shows; when the writes take well over their unloaded time (the shortest write after the thread had been waiting for a frame) and keep the thread busy, in 2 of 5 seconds, it measures how many frames a second the panel took, sends half that for a second so the store drains, then at most 85 % of it. After a few quiet seconds that most rises one frame a second, but never past the most frames a second the panel was sent for 8 quiet seconds in a row, on a T113 as on any panel, nor past a T113's 15 or 12, and only while the frames are no slower to write than they were then; once it has been lowered again after rising, it stays there. What it learned is forgotten when the panel starts again and when the frame rate setting changes. So a change is usually shown a frame or two after it is written, but a panel's store can still fill: a panel other than a T113 once when it is sent more than it shows from the start (until that is seen: a few seconds, longer if it is only a little slower), and any panel once after a rise, when it shows the content of the moment more slowly than the most it rises to; a change then waits behind the store until it drains. The frame rate setting stays the most a panel is sent. A frame that didn't change isn't sent, and the last one is sent again every second.
- The screen goes off (backlight off, a black frame, the stream's end) whenever a panel stops: turned off, unplugged, its profile deleted, or the engine quitting (at most 3 s for every panel together, counted from the quit). A deleted profile leaves the panel off, its profile kept, until a profile with that id comes back. Changing a running panel's profile moves it to the new one without turning the screen off.
- **Frames as JPEG.** A panel's `encoding` is `auto` (the default), `jpeg` or `raw`. `auto` streams JPEG to the panels it has been seen to work on: a BeadaPanel on the T113 platform with PanelLink 2 or later (the BeadaPanel 8 and its 1920 × 480 kin); every other panel gets raw RGB565, as 1.4 sends it. Each frame is one JPEG at quality 90, encoded from the turned and fitted frame, and the stop's black frame is a JPEG too. On a BeadaPanel 8 that is about 35 frames a second against raw's 16, for some 3–4 ms of encoding a frame. A panel that doesn't take the JPEG stream's start has its stream reset, as at the start, and is sent raw instead, with one line in the log, and stays raw until the engine restarts. `jpeg` tries JPEG on any panel, for one that isn't known yet; `raw` never does. After a JPEG stream ends, a BeadaPanel 8 takes no new stream for 20 to 30 s, so a panel turned off and on again, or set to another encoding, starts again 30 s after its JPEG stream ended, its state `starting`, with reason `settling`, until then (another engine started on the panel sooner retries until the panel answers).
- Unplugged, a panel stays saved; plugged in again it is found and runs again: in any port when it has a serial of its own, at the port it was saved at when it has none (or shares its serial with another saved panel).
- The log shows each panel starting, its frame rate and time a frame 10 s after it starts and then every 5 minutes, and why one failed (it is tried again every second). It also has a line for each change of a profile's document (`Profile …: changed (Commit) rev 12`), one when a panel writes the first frame of a new revision (`Panel …: wrote rev 12, encode+write 6.1 ms`), and one when a panel's pace changes by more than a fifth, at most every 5 s (`Panel …: pacing 9 fps (writes 48.3 ms, unloaded 4.5 ms)`).

## `devices.json`

The saved panels are in [`devices.json`](data-folder.md#the-panels) in the data folder:

```json
{"format": "infopanel.devices/1", "devices": [
  {"id": "0f8e4a52-6c1b-4f38-9a57-2d1c3b4e5f60", "family": "beadapanel", "model": "8", "serial": "934072005c00481401044f2420751fd6",
   "port": "0-1", "enabled": false, "profileId": null, "rotation": 0, "brightness": 100, "encoding": "auto"}
]}
```

Clients read and change it through the engine's devices API ([api.md](../../spec/docs/api.md#devices)): `GET /api/devices` lists the saved panels with their state and settings, `PATCH /api/devices/{id}` changes a panel's `enabled`, `profileId`, `rotation` and `brightness` (saved and applied at once), and `DELETE /api/devices/{id}` forgets a panel (its screen goes off; still plugged in, it comes back as a new panel, off). `POST /api/devices` saves a panel that need not be plugged in; one a client brings over from 1.x carries `v1`, the 1.x device's `deviceId` and `deviceLocation`, kept in the file, so the same 1.x device is never saved twice. `encoding` is set only in the file.

## Turning a panel on

A client turns a panel on with `PATCH /api/devices/{id}`, its `enabled` `true` and its `profileId` the profile to show. Without a client, in the file:

1. Plug the panel in with the engine running, and wait for its `found … saved, off` line in the log.
2. Quit the engine.
3. In `devices.json`, set the panel's `enabled` to `true` and its `profileId` to the id of the profile to show (the profile's folder name under `profiles/`). Optionally set `rotation` (0, 90, 180, 270), `brightness` (0–100) and `encoding` (`auto`, `jpeg`, `raw`).
4. Start the engine: the panel lights up within a second or two.

A file the engine can't read (not JSON, a value out of range) is left as it is, and panels stay off for that run, with the reason in the log. An `encoding` it doesn't know reads as `auto`.
