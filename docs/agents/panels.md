# USB panels

- **USB panels** (`InfoPanel.Core/Panels/`, docs/2.0/panels.md; BeadaPanel so far): libusb-1.0 through the engine's own function-pointer binding (`Usb/LibUsb`, loaded by full path: beside the engine, then `INFOPANEL_LIBUSB_DIR` (a full path only), then `/opt/homebrew/lib` and `/usr/local/lib` on macOS, `libusb-1.0.so.0` on Linux; none found: panels are unsupported, one log line) behind the seam `Usb/IUsbBackend` (a fake in the tests, `InfoPanel.Core.Tests/Panels/Usb/FakeUsbBackend.cs`, linked into the host tests). Devices are opened only through `UsbClaim`: the listed device checked again at its bus and address, configuration 1 set only when none is, interface 0 claimed, never a reset or a kernel-driver detach, so another holder fails `busy` or `access` with nothing written. `BeadaPanel/`: InfoPanel 1.4's protocol byte for byte (`BeadaPanelProtocol`, the info reply `BeadaPanelInfo`, the 21-model table, `BeadaPanelSession`'s start, frames, brightness and screen-off: every screen-off write tried but after an unplug, 3 s for all). JPEG frames (`PanelEncoding`, `devices.json`'s `encoding`: `auto` by default, `jpeg`, `raw`; any other value reads `auto`): `auto` is JPEG only where `BeadaPanelProtocol.TakesJpeg` says (platform 5, T113, with PanelLink 2 or later: the only panels seen decoding it, since nothing documents a capability), else raw, which is 1.4's stream unchanged; a JPEG session sends the type-5 `image/jpeg` start tag, each frame as one q90 JPEG (`PanelJpeg`, SkiaSharp's encoder, from `PanelTransform`'s opaque RGBA, so turned and fitted), and on stop a black JPEG and the type-6 end. A JPEG start tag that isn't taken (any failure but the panel gone) is told to the runner at once (so a start that then fails or is cancelled still counts), followed by the start's own recovery (PanelLinkReset, 1000 ms, the backlight) and the raw tag in the same session, logged, and `PanelManager` keeps that panel raw until the engine restarts; another `encoding` restarts the session. A T113 panel takes no new stream for 20 to 30 s after a JPEG one ends (the end tag or a PanelLinkReset alike), so `PanelManager` starts a panel again only `JpegSettle` (30 s) after its last JPEG session ended, state `waiting` until then; the wait is the physical panel's, kept by its USB device when its settings are forgotten. An unknown stored `encoding` is kept (`SavedPanel.UnknownEncoding`) and written back until the encoding is set. `PanelManager` (one thread of its own; `PanelService`, a hosted service registered after the render service, starts it unless `--no-panels`) lists USB devices every second, identifies a new BeadaPanel by its info query, matches it to `devices.json` (`PanelStore`) by serial (saved panels sharing a serial by serial and port together; still ambiguous: saved as new, logged), else by port; a panel matching none is taken by a saved panel without a serial that was never plugged in (port null: a client's save) only when exactly one such saved panel of its model exists, it is the only panel of its model found unmatched in that pass (every one counted, serial clashes too), its serial doesn't clash, it isn't a panel forgotten (`DELETE`) this run (each forgotten panel is remembered by every identity it had, its serial and its last port, and a panel is refused when either of its own matches), and the memory of those (`PanelManager.MaxForgotten`, 256 identities, none ever dropped) isn't full (full: no panel is adopted for the rest of the run, logged once) (`PanelManager.Adopt`), else it saves a new one off without a profile, and runs a `PanelRunner` (a thread per panel) for each connected, enabled panel whose profile is ready. A timer's pass is dropped while one is queued or running. A profile change moves the runner's sink to the new profile, the session kept. The runner's `PanelSink` asks for scale 1 at the `frameRate` setting, lowered to the panel's pace (`PanelPacer`, a new one each session and each change of the setting, checked once a second: at most the ceiling, a T113's (platform 5) 15 fps JPEG and 12 raw, under its measured rates, none on other panels; at most 0.85 × 1000 / the mean frame time, the write plus the encode on a JPEG stream; once writes are seen held back by a full device buffer, in 2 of 5 checks, against the least write that followed an idle wait, one check at half the measured rate, then a learned 0.85 × that rate; the learned pace rises 1 fps a quiet check, after 3, up to the highest pace that kept up for 8 quiet checks (a T113's ceiling only bounds it) while the idle writes are no longer than then, and stays where a lowering after a rise puts it), without bounds, and the runner's thread keeps to the lower of the setting and the pace itself at every write (its next write begins no sooner than one such interval after the last began, with the newest frame); `OnFrame` copies into one of two buffers as the one pending frame and skips a frame whose content hash, size and rotation equal the last; the runner transforms it (`PanelTransform`: rotation, fit, RGB565, or RGBA it encodes once as JPEG), writes it, re-sends the last frame after 1 s without a new one, and sends brightness between frames. A stop of any kind (off, unplugged, profile gone, quit) turns the screen off, no transfer but the screen-off's started once it is asked, within 3 s of the ask (a quit asks every running panel at once and stops a pass before its next query, 3 s from the quit for all and the manager's thread); a failed session starts again on the next pass with its reason kept. Packaging ships libusb: `native/libusb/build.sh` builds the Mac app's arm64 `libusb-1.0.dylib` from libusb's pinned release source only (`native/libusb/pins.txt`: version and SHA-256s), for the app's minimum macOS (`Info.plist`'s `LSMinimumSystemVersion`; a newer `minos` is refused); `packaging/macos/build.sh` runs it and copies the library into the publish before its layout step, so it lands in `Contents/MacOS`; `packaging/windows/build.ps1` puts the release's x64 `libusb-1.0.dll` in the publish beside the engine; both packages carry libusb's licence text, the source's `COPYING`, as `libusb-COPYING.txt` (docs/2.0/packaging.md; the terms: LICENSES.md). The API (`Api/DeviceEndpoints.cs`, the `devices` feature, spec/docs/api.md "Devices"): `GET /api/devices`, every token's (`supported` false with `PanelService.Unsupported`: `off`, `library` or `store`), and the session token's `POST /api/devices` (a panel saved that need not be plugged in, as given: `family` (one the engine drives, else 422 `device.family`), `model` (one of the family's, else `device.model`), `serial` or none, `v1` (the 1.x `deviceId` and `deviceLocation` it is brought over from, stored as given in `devices.json`'s `v1` (`PanelV1Identity`) and answered in its entry when the strings fit the schema) or none, `settings` as a change gives them; `SaveAsync`, written before it is listed and answering the entry as saved, in the same turn of the manager's thread; 409 `existsProblem` with the saved panel's `id` for a `v1.deviceId` saved already (ordinal, ignoring case; checked first) or a serial of the family saved already, 409 `problem` where no manager runs or the file can't be held or written; 201 with its entry, `notConnected`, port null), `PATCH /api/devices/{id}` (settings merged through `UpdateAsync`; 409 while `devices.json` can't be held or a change can't be written to it (written before it is applied), 422 `device.setting` for a setting the family doesn't honour) and `DELETE` (`ForgetAsync`: screen off, removed, and a connected panel identified again as new once its session has ended). `PanelManager.Version` counts the list's changes, checked after each of its actions (a change of the stats alone at most once a second, `StatsInterval`) and raised as `Changed`, which every `/ws` connection sends as a `devices` message. Sleep and wake (`Power/`): the seam `IPowerEvents` (through `PowerEvents.WaitForSuspending`, the one way in, `SleepArriving` is called on the source's notification thread and returns the sleep's work, which the source waits for at most 3 s before it lets the computer sleep; `Resumed` records the wake; sleep work that runs after a later wake does nothing), and `PanelPower` over it: before the sleep `PanelManager.SuspendAsync` asks every running panel to stop at once (screen off, 3 s for all) and holds passes and starts (a pass under way stops before its next query); 1 s after the wake `ResumeAsync` runs a pass (a sleep again within that second keeps them dark), the time asleep counted toward `JpegSettle` (the manager's clock stops while the Mac sleeps). macOS's source is `MacPowerEvents`: IOKit's `IORegisterForSystemPower` on a CoreFoundation run loop on a thread of its own (`Power`), will-sleep allowed (`IOAllowPowerChange`) once the panels are off or at 3 s, an idle sleep's question allowed at once; `PanelService` starts it after the manager (`PanelServiceOptions.Power`; none on other platforms yet) and disposes it, unregistered, before the panels stop at quit or session end.
- **Every other family** (docs/2.0/panels.md, plugins.md "Panel packages"): a hosted driver gets the device seam through its constructor (`PanelHostEntry.Load` prefers a public constructor taking `IPanelDevices`, given the host's `Devices/EnginePanelDevices`, else the parameterless one). `EnginePanelDevices`: USB over `IUsbBackend` and `UsbClaim` (the interface asked for, its endpoints from `IUsbBackend.Describe`, libusb's cached configuration descriptor, without which the open is refused; a transfer only on the claimed interface's bulk endpoints in the call's direction, any other answering `NotSupported` with nothing sent; `IUsbDevice.WriteZeroLengthPacket` only when the driver asks and the write fills its last packet, in what is left of the call's deadline, the token and the handle looked at between; reads in `ReadSlice`s gathering what comes until the buffer is full, a short packet ends it or the deadline passes, so a cancel or dispose ends them), HID through `IHidSystem` (`HidSharpSystem`: HidSharp's exclusivity layer across processes, so the engine's own hosts never share a panel; no OS share mode, so on Windows another program can open the same collection, as in 1.4.x; feature reports kept to their timeout, the stream closed past it) and `HidChooser` (ids, then the port where the OS gives one, Linux sysfs and macOS IORegistry paths, with the USB device's bus and address (`HidEntry.Bus`, `Address`: sysfs `busnum` and `devnum`, IOKit's `USB Address`; an entry with a port but no bus or address is refused, `busy`, logged once, and fails `IsStill`); else the collections grouped by their physical device, Windows' USB parent from CfgMgr, the driver's filter first, and each panel given one device, reserved, a single one too, kept while a collection of it is open, released at its close, dropped only by a listing of its own ids; `ForCollection` takes a collection by id only within the device last given that panel, taken again after a release; after the open `IsStill` checks a fresh listing, another device in its place closed, `gone`), serial through `ISerialSystem` (`SerialPorts`, `System.IO.Ports`) and `SerialPortFinder` (the device's own port only, its identity checked: Linux sysfs ids, `busnum` and `devnum`; macOS `ioreg -a` location, ids and `USB Address`; Windows SetupAPI's present USB devices of the ids (`WindowsPorts.Devices`) with the COM ports below each and each one's location path (`SPDRP_LOCATION_PATHS`): the only one when its port chain (`SerialPortFinder.WindowsPortChain`, the `USB(n)` hops after `USBROOT`) is the candidate's port path's and, when it has a Windows serial and the candidate's can be read, the two match (no location path: `busy`, `WindowsNoPort`; another port or serial: `gone`; the same ids in the same port is the same panel, documented), else the one whose instance id is the candidate's serial (Windows' unique-id capability; the serial read through libusb, opened unclaimed, only then), several that can't be told apart `busy` with `SerialPortFinder.WindowsIdenticalDevices` logged once; another device there now is `gone`; opened with DTR and RTS off (`ISerialSystem.Open`), checked from the opened port (`Verify`: Linux the descriptor's `statx` device up `/sys/dev/char` to its USB device, `LinuxOwner`; macOS `fstat` against the callout node's `stat`, `MacOSOwner`; Windows the COM name, `WindowsOwner`), only then, with the token and the open's deadline looked at again (past either: closed, `timeout`), `IOpenedSerial.SetLines`; `OpenedSerial` over `ISerialLine`, its dispose discarding both buffers and closing on a worker within `DisposeWait`; pure functions over the OS's tables, tested per OS with fakes). Every open (`DeviceOpen.Run`) runs within the call's one deadline and token (the descriptor lookup, the HID listing and the finder's `ioreg` too), and every handle's call takes its lock within its own deadline (`DeviceCall.Enter`, a `SemaphoreSlim`: past it `timeout`, a cancel while waiting throws, the handle's dispose (its `_disposing` token) answers the waiting calls `io` at once) and gives the device only what is left (`EnginePanelDevices.Deadlines`, the tests' scripted clock; a zero-length packet's time read once, none left `timeout`): past it `timeout`, cancelled it throws, and a handle that opens late is disposed. A dispose closes a HID stream or serial port at once, ending the call under way with `io`; a USB write (libusb's synchronous transfer, no cancel; asynchronous transfers with cancel are a larger backend change, not made) ends at its own timeout and closes the device then, a new open meanwhile `busy`; a dispose returns within `DisposeWait` (200 ms) either way. Both packages are referenced by `InfoPanel.Core` (versions in `Directory.Packages.props`, licences in `LICENSES.md`). Frames: `PanelRunner` writes the first of a session's formats through a `PanelFrameWriter` (one a session): `PanelTransform.Render` (turned, fitted), `Dim` (when `DimInSoftware`, Skia's colour matrix as 1.4's `ApplyBrightness`; a brightness change renews the sink so the same picture comes again), `Overlay` (the PNG for the rotation, each checked by its header before it is decoded, `PanelFrameWriter.Problem`: the panel's own size, at most `MaxOverlayBytes` decoded), `Crop`, then `PanelJpeg.EncodeWithin` (a step lower to the floor, logged once; one still over at the floor is not written, `Encode` false, the last frame kept, and a keep-alive due still goes with it) or `Convert` (RGB565 big-endian, `Rgb565LeTruncated` (1.4.x Thermalright's little-endian), RGB888, BGR888 by truncation; `Rgb565Le` stays Skia's rounded conversion, BeadaPanel's, which is not truncation); a little-endian RGB565 session with none of them renders straight into its buffer as before. The keep-alive is the session's `KeepAliveEvery` when it gives one. `JpegSettle` is a BeadaPanel's only (`PanelManager.SettlesAfterJpeg`): another family's JPEG session opens again at once. A panel's own `frameRate` (`SavedPanel.FrameRate`, `devices.json`, 1–60 or absent) replaces the engine's in its runner's rate function (`Runtime.FrameRate`, read by its sink at every tick), still under its ceiling and pacer. Family settings: `panel.json`'s `settings` read by `PanelFamilySettings.Parse` into `PanelPackageFamily.Settings` (and the API's `deviceFamily.settings`); a save's or change's `familySettings` checked by `PanelFamilySettings.Apply` (422 `device.setting` at `/familySettings/{id}`, the schema's errors about one value too, `DeviceEndpoints.ErrorsOf`; an integer read as the formats read numbers and kept as the integer), kept as a compact object in `SavedPanel.FamilySettings` in the family's declared order (values of settings no longer declared kept after them; a change that changes nothing gives the stored text back, so it reopens nothing), passed to `Open` as `PanelOpenOptions.FamilySettings` (over the host pipe in the `open` request); a change stops the session and the next pass opens it with them. "Try it as…": a probe's model the family doesn't declare is saved by its code (no `modelName`); the driver answers `notSupported` until the `model` setting (type `model`, values the family's models) names one. A model's `v1Aliases` (`PanelPackageModel.V1Aliases`, `PanelPackageFamily.ModelOfV1`) map a 1.x panel saved under an older name. `PanelPackages.ReadManifest` reads `panel.json` through the SDK's `ManifestFile` (one handle opened without following a link; the SDK's internals are visible to `InfoPanel.Core` for it).
